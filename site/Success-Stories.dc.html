Success Stories

Real people who found their footing.

People we have worked with, kept general at their request. The situations are exact; the identifying details are not.

Career changer
Who they were

Fifteen years in logistics operations, wanting to move into project management before another restructure decided it for them.

The struggle

Eleven months of applications, almost no replies. Every rejection read as a verdict on the whole career.

What we did

An advisor rewrote how the operations work was described, then made two direct introductions inside the network.

Where they are now

Programme manager at a regional distributor. Started in March.

Returning after a gap
Who they were

A treasury analyst who stepped out for four years to raise two children.

The struggle

Assumed she would have to restart at the bottom, and was applying to roles two grades below her last one.

What we did

Matched her with a mentor who had made the same return, and reframed the gap as four years of unbroken competence.

Where they are now

Back in treasury at her previous level, four days a week.

New to the region
Who they were

A civil engineer who relocated to the Gulf with a spouse's posting, with eight years of experience elsewhere.

The struggle

No local references, no idea which firms were actually hiring, and a portfolio that read as foreign.

What we did

Explained how his projects would be read here, and put him in front of three consultancies in our network.

Where they are now

Senior engineer on an infrastructure programme, seven weeks after his first call.

Experienced job seeker
Who they were

A commercial director in his fifties, made redundant when his division was folded into another.

The struggle

Reaching final rounds and losing to cheaper, younger candidates. Beginning to consider retiring early by default.

What we did

Pointed him at firms that needed exactly his judgement, and told two of them so directly.

Where they are now

Commercial lead at a family group turning professional. Not retired.

Recent graduate
Who they were

A marketing graduate, one year out, working retail shifts to cover rent.

The struggle

Around two hundred applications, four interviews, no offer. Every entry role wanted two years of experience.

What we did

Built a case out of her university campaign work, ran two mock interviews, and vouched for her to a founder.

Where they are now

Marketing associate at an eight-person company. Off the shift rota.

Career changer
Who they were

A secondary school teacher of nine years, wanting to move into corporate learning and development.

The struggle

Told repeatedly that teaching was not commercial experience, and had begun to believe it.

What we did

A mentor from an L&D function spent three sessions translating the classroom into the language of capability building.

Where they are now

Learning designer at a professional services firm.
